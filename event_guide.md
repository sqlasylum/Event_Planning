# Quick Start Guide for Events

How to USE this Document, 

The goal of this document is to walk you through questions, ideas and suggestions related to hosting an event.  It is not a Direct walkthrough for your event but it will guide you on many of the discussions you will need to have.  The Menu below will link you off to different parts of the Document to make it easier.  

You are welcome to COPY this document and store it locally on your google drive or download it as a PDF or other format.  This is offered free to use for the community.

# Events

List of items to start with. 

1. Find a couple of passionate volunteers and members of the community. Get together and discuss what you aim to achieve for the event—key items to be listed below.   
   1. Location?   
   2. Length of event?  You should always start with the smallest event you can for your first event,  having a one-day or even half-day is a good way to start.  Don’t go for a multi-day event out of the gate.   
   3. Time of year?   
   4. Time of Week?   
   5. Possible \# of attendees   
   6. Number of Sessions   
   7. Start and end time.   
2. Location will be your first item while you have the numbers above.  It will determine your date as well.  Once you have a location other things can be decided.  Key items to consider on location.   
   1. Cost,  Free is great if you can get it.   
   2. Can it support the number of people you are looking at?   
   3. Do you have multiple rooms for multiple sessions?  You Can do just one room and one set of sessions just make sure to plan accordingly.   
   4. Is the location good for access?  Parking? Hotels nearby?  If it’s a College Campus make sure you find out about parking.   
   5. If you can do weekend events you might find more venues open to you but they may have higher costs to have weekend staff.    
3. Location will determine the date of the event.  You have to use what is available to you on dates, Once you have that secured you can start to promote.    
   1. Make sure to get some sort of signed contract/relationship with the venue.  You don’t want that changing on you.   
   2. You might need to get event insurance for your event. You will need to ask your venue this.  This is usually a couple hundred dollars for a 1 day event.  You can use sites like eventhelper.com for insurance.    
4. Location will help to decide budget as well which is important 

Once you have the location and date everything else listed below can and should be run in parallel. 

# Sponsors

This section will cover sponsors, how to obtain and sell, and Determining costs in the budgeting section.  

1. Sponsors   
   1. Reach out to the salespeople that try and sell you something. Via linkedin, Email, twitter or wherever they reach out to you.    
   2. Find local tech hiring firms in your area.   
   3. Find local software/tech companies in your area that might be trying to hire people.   
2. Create a prospectus of what you are offering to the sponsors. This can be in Pdf/Doc or power point form.   
   1. Examples of this are in the docs  
   2. Make a matrix of benefits and the levels. Make it easy to visually see what is available.   
   3. Be clear on pricing.   
   4. Offer add-ons if applicable   
3. Create a contract that can be signed by you and the sponsor.   
   1. An example of this can be found in the docs.   
   2. Make sure to have an authorized organizer of the event sign the document along with a representative from the sponsor company as well.   
4. Setup Paypal/Venmo to take donations.   
   1. If you have a 501c(3) company or know of one that can take money for you, that is best.  Since you won’t have to show the money directly as income.   
   2. An alternative to taking direct donations is working with the sponsors to have them buy the items needed. Like the sponsors directly buy the food for lunch and so forth.   
   3. Be wary of allowing sponsors to directly pay for items. This event is not as critical to them as it is to you.  You as the organizers are the face of the event and it will reflect on you if something is missing. Not the sponsors.    
5. Communication   
   1. Example marketing emails are in the docs.   
   2. Make sure to email/chat with your sponsors often.   
   3. Let them know about what the space looks like.   
   4. If you can take pictures of the space and share that as an album with the sponsors, this is very helpful. Most event spaces have example pictures   
   5. Getting on a call with sponsors and jumping onto video calls is a great way to build a relationship with the sponsors.  Even if it’s just to discuss how the event is going to go.   
   6. It’s good to create a list of detailed items the sponsors need to know about an event. Many times you are talking to one person but other people are going to be on site.  If you can provide an easy copy/paste block of text that can be sent to onsite personnel it helps greatly.  Examples in the doc.   
   7. Tell your sponsors setup/take down times.  Make sure they are aware of when they can setup booth.   
6. Dedicate someone to managing the sponsors   
   1. Sponsors have a good amount of questions and need help with setup when they arrive on site.  It’s good to have an organizer who is dedicated to them and not helping with morning registration or some other task.    
   2. This person should have the list of sponsors and be ready to “check-in” the sponsors and show them where they need to go for the event.

# Volunteers

	Volunteers are the heart of an event,  Making sure they are informed and feel valued is very important. 

1. Communication is critical   
   1. Talk with your volunteers often.   
   2. Schedule calls frequently before the event.   
   3. Create 1 page/information sheet with key information about the event so the volunteers can be prepared at the event  
   4. Use telegram, Slack, or even group text chat to keep conversation open during the event.   
2. Assign a dedicated volunteer coordinator   
3. Collect Key information about volunteers.   
   1. Name, Email, Phone number(for texting and checking in).   
4. Have backups in critical positions.   
   1. Morning registration is critical and you should make sure you have dependable volunteers for that portion of the event. It’s important to teach these volunteers either the night before or the morning of on how to do registration.  They are the first face the event normally sees and will answer most of the questions around the event.  It is important to have them well-trained in what is needed.   
   2. Make sure to synch up and connect with your volunteers often before the event.   
   3. Things come up, Things can go wrong, make sure the volunteers have your information (Cell,Email,Slack,Telegram) and can reach out to your volunteer coordinator. 

# Sessions/Program Committee

Content is critical for your event; attendees want to interact with speakers and other attendees directly and want to learn something as well.  The sessions and schedule you build will directly impact whether people want to attend the event.  

1. Build a committee  
   1. Think Local,  Content and sessions can be very specific to an area, region, and technology.  Know what your event is about and what the goal of the event is, this will allow you to plan accordingly.  The guidelines below are guidelines only and not hard fast rules.   
   2. Choose at least 3-5 people who can vote on sessions and provide information about what to look for.    
   3. Make sure your committee is diverse in experience level and technologies they are interested in.  It is important to have a diversity of thought for your program committee.   
   4. Have the committee Rank the session solely based on the session and not the speaker. Most tools will allow you to rate the session without revealing the speaker. This helps you focus on the topic instead of the person speaking.   
   5. Publish dates around Call for papers.   
      1. Open Date.   
      2. Close Date.   
      3. Acceptance/Communication to speakers date (This is an estimate).   
      4. Schedule Publish Date(Estimate again based on above).   
   6. If you don’t get the number of sessions you want by the close date, send out a community message extending the close date, and make sure the speakers who have already submitted this might change the acceptance/communication updates.   
2. Speakers   
   1. Specifically invite speakers to your event,  Sending out a general call for papers is good but if you want to grow the community and want to see specific speakers at the event, feel free to direct email and invite them.    
   2. Plan for cancellations,  You should have a waiting list of session ready to go.  I have yet to run an event where I did not have a speaker cancel a session for many various reasons.  If you have an event where people are traveling this is even more critical   
   3. Make sure you get Speakers to “confirm” the session. Sometimes the speaker won’t remember they submitted and are unaware of being accepted.  It’s critical to receive a confirmation from them.   
   4. Ask them what is the preferred session they have for the event.  Many speakers have a specific session they would LOVE to give.  This doesn’t mean it will be used but it helps you to stay informed on what the speaker is most passionate about.    
3. Session  
   1. Don’t think everything has to be a technical session,  Professional development is a key skill of any good professional so make sure to foster nontechnical sessions as well.   
   2. When growing a community and starting an event give maximum possibilities to new speakers, this means my event may not have the best technical sessions but it has the most diversity and it will grow the most in the future.  Do this by allowing each speaker one session until every speaker has at least one session.  So even if the best speaker has 3 sessions submitted they only get one session until you have made sure everyone else has a session as well.  Once everyone has a session start going to the second session for those speakers. You can use a voting system to ask the community what sessions they are most interested in and you can plan the second session on those votes.  This means you will always have new speakers coming in.  If you submit to one of the events you have a very high chance of being accepted as long as we have space in the schedule.    
   3. When you have two sessions with the same topic but different presenters.  
      1. If you have voting you can focus on higher votes or ranking to determine the one to keep the session.   
      2. If someone hasn’t submitted multiple sessions but the other speaker has then see the rule above about only getting one session to start with.   
      3. Reach out to the speakers and see if they have a preference in another session. This could give them an option to say they really wanted to present X session instead of the duplicated session.    
4. Keynotes   
   1. Considerations for Keynotes   
      1. Do you have a space to hold everyone at one time?   
      2. Do you have something you want to say to everyone? (quick morning announcements can be done in each starting session room).    
      3. Do sponsors need to speak? Part of the Sponsor package?   
      4. Do you have a group/session that will draw a crowd and everyone will want to see it?   
   2. Opening/Closing sessions,   
      1. If you have giveaways for everyone, it’s a good idea to have some sort of closing event. This is a good way to get survey feedback.  You are not entered until you fill out a survey.   
      2. Closing time is good to tell everyone about the next event, ask for feedback, and generally get the community together before you go.   
      3. If you already have a keynote, you can make opening announcements easily. If you don’t have a keynote do these opening announcements in each session room. Recruit others to make the announcements. 

5. Schedule   
   1. Determine Start time and Stop time.   
      1. Based on Location, Some areas are better at starting early and ending early, Others are better at starting late and ending late.   
      2. Typical start times, 9:00 am, 9:30 am, 8:30 am.   
      3. Always give time for Registration/check in.  i.e. (Registration opens at 8:00 am and sessions start at 9:00 am).   
      4. Typical Stop times, 5:00 pm, 4:30 pm, 5:30pm.   
   2. Determine Session length.   
      1. 45 minutes is common. Suggested (40 minutes content 5 min Q\&A)  
      2. 60 minutes is common, Suggested (45 minutes content 15 min Q\&A)  
      3. This should be based on how many sessions you want and what your community wants.    
      4. There is no right or wrong answer to the session length.    
      5. Be consistent if possible with session length, it will make the schedule easier and less confusing.   
   3. Determine the time between sessions.   
      1. If you are in a close space with everything nearby, 10 minutes works well.   
      2. If you are across campus or far apart from sessions plan 15 minutes.   
      3. This is location and community-specific. You can also change this depending on coffee breaks and how much networking time you have at other points during the day.   
   4. Determine coffee breaks if any.   
      1. Having a sponsor for these items helps.   
      2. New small events may not have the budget for things like this but larger events should consider an afternoon break. It’s needed for the attendees to stay attentive.

# Location Scouting Checklist

You won’t have all these items at the Location, This is just a long list of things you should look up and answer. You might find many of these answers just when talking about the location or when reading through the materials from that specific location. 

1. Get pictures   
   1. Hallways   
   2. Exits   
   3. Entrances   
   4. Session rooms   
   5. If they have Example photos from past events ask for them   
   6. If they have a current event going on ask if you can take pictures of the layout  
   7. Building itself(great for giving directions)   
   8. The turn/Road to get into the building(again Directions)   
2. Check distances (bring Tape Measure)  
   1. If you have two pillars either ask or measure if you can put a table between them.   
   2. Check space for walking paths in the hall and ADA-accessible situations.   
3. Validate room setup,   
   1. Understand what Theater style is, vs classroom. Get room sizes, Most locations already have this in a printout.   
   2. Do they have podiums up front for presenters?  Is that extra?  
   3. Where do presenters plug in laptops?    
   4. What types of connectors do they have?   
   5. What kind of Mic/Sound do they have?   
   6. Does the room need a mic? If it’s small probably not.   
4. Bathrooms  
   1.   How many do they have?  Is that enough for the size of event?   
   2. Do they Refresh/Maintain during the event? Extra cost to do so?   
5. Can you place anything outside the building?  Flags?  Yard signs for directions?   
6. Parking   
   1. Free?   
   2. Vouchers?   
   3. Paid?   
   4. Can you get discounts?   
   5. Can you pay something to give all attendees a pass?   
7. Costs  
   1. Usually, they have a specific cost sheet for all items.  Make sure to have this from the brochure.  Things like a/v are additional.   
8. Janitorial Services  
   1. Does the event need to empty the garbage?   
   2. Break down boxes?   
   3. Rules around putting up chairs?   
   4. Moving tables? 

**General Questions** 

1. What times do you have the building for setup?   
   1. How early can you get in?  
   2. When do you have to be out?   
   3. Setup the night before?   
2. Insurance needed?   
   1. How much?   
   2. What name on it?   
   3. What requirements does it have?   
3. Can we ship items to the location?    
   1. If so how soon before?   
   2. If not do they have a fedex nearby?   
   3. How long will they hold items?   
   4. Will they sign for items?   
4. Catering options and outside food?   
   1. This is a larger discussion but the general question is can you bring outside food in the morning like bagels/coffee?   
   2. Can we bring snacks in the speaker room and cases of water?   
   3. Can people eat in the session rooms?   
   4. Do they have a Dedicated lunchroom?   
   5. Eat outside?  Depending on the Weather?   
   6. Can we provide the snack service in the afternoon?  (placing out cookies/granola bars).   
   7. Are water stations available?  Extra cost or included?   
5. Registration  
   1. Where will it be?   
   2. Does it have power?   
   3. Do we need to bring tables/tables provided?   
   4. Do tables cost extra? Included?   
6. Vendors  
   1. Power included?   
   2. Table cloth included on tables?   
   3. Costs for extra tables/chairs?   
   4. Can Vendors bring food? Donuts/cookies/treats?  
7. Signs around the location? 	  
   1. Can you put things on the walls? Blue tape?   
   2. Room signs on the doors?   
   3. Directional signs? Do they have whiteboard arrows or something that shows directions for attendees?   
   4. Easels available? Extra cost?   
   5. If they have LCD screens can you use them? Extra cost or included?   
8. Recording   
   1. Is it ok to record sessions at the location?   
   2. Extra Cost?   
   3. Can attendees/speakers record sessions? Not the organizers   
   4. Any special restrictions on cameras/video?   
9. Wifi   
   1. Is wifi available?   
   2. Free/Paid?   
   3. Can it handle X amount of people?   
   4. Can vendors purchase additional bandwidth if needed?   
   5. Can we customize the wifi name? (Vendor Selling point)
