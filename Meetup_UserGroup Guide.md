  
Quick Start Guide For Meetups

Key items for Meetups 

1. Be consistent. Even if you have 1-2 people at the meetings, continue to meet. Try not to miss any meetings in the first year as it helps to grow the community to keep meeting regularly. A meeting with one person can still be productive.   
2. You are growing a community, not just a single group. Network with the people attending, get to know them and know what they are working on.   
3. In the beginning, introduce everyone. Give everyone a chance for introductions.  When you get a larger group, you can just focus on the people that want to talk about jobs/issues.  
4. Ask the community if they have any specific problems they are trying to face at that time.  Offer the whole group to answer.  
5. Never underestimate how much just having food can bring people to a meetup.   
6. Survey your community. This is easy enough through Google Forms. Have sponsors help with giveaways to get responses.  

List of items to start with:

1. Find a couple of passionate volunteers and members of the community. Get together and discuss what you aim to achieve for the meetup.   
2. Find an overall organization that can assist you.  
   1. PG US   
   2. PASS Association   
   3. PG EU  
3. Determine the frequency to meet.   
   1. Start slow, Monthly is the most common format but if you are worried about keeping up with demand Quarterly is an option. You will grow much slower at quarterly though. It will be hard to maintain momentum.  
   2. Determine the day of the week  
      1. Survey the group.   
      2. Base this off the location you are in.   
      3. Don’t do Mondays or Fridays.  
   3. Determine the time of day  
      1. Evenings?   
      2. Afternoons?   
      3. Example time: 3-5 pm. I just leave slightly early from work but still have time at home with my family.   
      4. If you do evenings, consider things like commute time/traffic.   
4. Find Speakers.   
   1. Be prepared to speak at the first few events.   
   2. Reach out to speakers from other events.  
   3. Reach out to other user groups asking for speakers.   
   4. Don’t be afraid of presentations that are not fully related to your group. It’s ok to have a PG user group that does a presentation on DataBricks or Snowflake if it relates to what your audience is working on. Building a community is not about just one technology.   
5. Location  
   1. Have good internet at the location if you intend to broadcast/record.   
   2. Think Local, you will know the area and know how hard it is to get to someplace in traffic, weather, or special events. Think about what area works.   
   3. If you can keep one location for a long time, it really helps with attendance.  Attendees will be used to the location and know what it takes to go. This, combined with knowing the speaker, will tell them it’s worth it to attend.   
   4. Make sure your location allows networking time. If the meetup starts at 6:30 with a 1 hour presentation you won’t be leaving at 7:30. You need time for intros and for networking after the presentation. If your location can give you something like 6-8:00 pm that helps.   
   5. Many locations now require attendance lists for those in the building. In most cases, this is simply for check-in and security. Make it clear to your hosting location that they will not use this for advertising or mailing purposes.    
6. Sponsors  
   1. Reach out to the salespeople that try to sell you something, via Linkedin, Email, Twitter or wherever they reach out to you.   
   2. Find local tech hiring firms in your area.   
   3. Find local software/tech companies in your area that might be trying to hire people.   
   4. Don’t directly take money unless you are not for profit or willing to show it on taxes. Sponsors can easily buy food and have it delivered or picked up, they can also provide giveaways and other items without ever providing money to you directly.   
   5. Allow sponsors to network and connect with the attendees at the in-person meetups.   
   6. DO NOT give out your user list to sponsors.
